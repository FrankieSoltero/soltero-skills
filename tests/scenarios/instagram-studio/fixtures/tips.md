# Five tips for keeping a habit alive

## Shrink it until it is boring

The version of the habit you can do on your worst day is the real habit.
Pick the smallest unit that still counts — one page, one stretch, one
sentence — and let that be the whole requirement. A boring minimum survives
travel, deadlines and bad sleep; an ambitious one only survives good weeks.

## Attach it to something you already do

New habits do not need new willpower so much as a reliable cue. Hang the
habit off something that already happens without being remembered: the
kettle boiling, closing the laptop, brushing your teeth. The existing
routine does the remembering for you.

## Decide when, not just what

"Read more" has no moment in it, so it never arrives. Give the habit a
time and a place and it turns into something you can either do or skip,
which is a far easier thing to keep honest about than an intention.

## Never skip twice

One missed day is weather. Two missed days is the beginning of a new
pattern, and the third feels like permission. Treat the day after a miss as
the only day that really matters, and do even the shrunken version of the
habit to close it out.

## Track it where you can see it

A habit you keep in your head competes with everything else in your head.
Write it somewhere visible — a calendar square, a card on the fridge, an
app you open anyway — so that the record, not your memory, tells you how
you are doing.
